Evaluation Framework

Evaluation Metrics

The project uses four standard NLP evaluation metrics to assess summarization quality against human-written reference summaries.

🔵
ROUGE-1
Unigram overlap between generated and reference. Measures if same individual words appear. Best result: 0.418 (role_only)
🟣
ROUGE-2
Bigram (2-word sequence) overlap. More sensitive to fluency and word ordering. Best: 0.200 (role_only)
🟡
ROUGE-L
Longest Common Subsequence — a proxy for sentence-level structure and fluency. Best: 0.298 (role_only)
🟢
BLEU
N-gram precision with brevity penalty. Lowest scores, typical for abstractive summarization. Best: 0.135 (role_only)

Key Findings
✓ Role prefix helps most
The role_only variant consistently scores highest across all metrics, suggesting that structured persona priming is the single most effective optimization. Adding CoT or filler removal on top does not always compound the gains.
⚠ chunk_full underperforms
Chunk-based processing (recursive merge) yields lower ROUGE scores than direct summarization on long documents. This is likely due to the recursive second-pass compressing too aggressively with T5-small's 512-token limit. A stronger model (T5-base, FLAN-T5) would mitigate this.
→ CoT adds marginal benefit
Chain-of-thought prompting improves R1 vs. baseline (0.410 vs 0.410) but is nearly equivalent. T5-small may lack the capacity to truly leverage CoT reasoning steps; this technique shows stronger gains on larger models like GPT-3 or FLAN-T5-XXL.
∅ Filler removal is neutral
Removing filler words ("please", "just", "basically") from the instruction prompt has minimal measurable effect at this scale. T5-small appears robust to instruction noise. May show larger effects in few-shot or fine-tuned settings.
